4. IV & Endogeneity: The Room Light & Light-Blind Sensor

When the error u (the warm room light) is correlated with x (⟨x, u⟩ ≠ 0), OLS attributes the shadow P_x u to x and becomes biased. An instrument z that is blind to the error (z ⊥ u) yet sees the regressor (z ⊄ x) recovers β via oblique projection along z⊥.

5. GMM: Comparing Sensor Readings in the Space of Moments

With L = 2 sensors (instruments) and k = 1 parameter, the model's possible readings form a line col(D) = {Db} in ℝ². GMM minimizes ‖m - Db‖_W² = ḡ(b)'Wḡ(b), discounting noisier sensors via the weighting matrix W.

6. Summary: One Single Idea

All linear estimators—OLS, GLS, IV, 2SLS, and GMM—are close geometric relatives. They all project the data vector onto a subspace; the only thing that changes from one estimator to the next is the inner product matrix A used to measure distance.

The Unifying Master Equation
β̂ = argmin_b (y − Xb)' A (y − Xb) ⟺ X'A(y − Xβ̂) = 0 ⟺ β̂ = (X'AX)⁻¹ X'Ay
Each estimator is a judge, and each judge is an inner product. Click any row below to jump directly to its interactive laboratory.

The Logical Thread, in Six Steps

1Vector Space

Data (y, columns of X and Z) are vectors in ℝⁿ. Adding or rescaling variables keeps you inside ℝⁿ.

2Subspace & Span

All possible linear predictions {Xb} form the column space col(X), automatically closed under addition and scaling.

3Basis & Dimension

Linearly independent columns guarantee unique coefficients (identification): k parameters, n − k degrees of freedom.

4Inner Product

Defines length, distance, angle, and orthogonality. Choosing A is an econometric choice—best when matched to the noise.

5Projection

The closest point in col(X) has an A-orthogonal residual, turning minimization into linear normal equations.

6OLS, GLS, IV, 2SLS, GMM

One single projection problem with different subspaces and notions of distance—all unified in one formula.
